## 2. Artificial Intelligence in the Judiciary

### Overview & Mains Context
**Instructor:** In Judiciary topics for UPSC Mains, there is usually one of two dimensions:
1. A typical question about **Judicial Independence vs. Judicial Accountability** (a recurring theme in Mains).
2. Recent technological or structural developments related to the Judiciary.

Every year, there is one question from the Judiciary in Mains — either a 10-marker or a 15-marker. For instance, last year's question linked Judicial Independence and Judicial Accountability with Constitutional Morality. Earlier, there were questions on the Collegium system, judicial appointments, and women's representation in the judiciary. 

Similarly, **Artificial Intelligence in the Judiciary** is a heavily debated and relevant topic.

### Introductory Framing & Immediate Context
**How to introduce an answer on AI in Judiciary:**
- **Problem Statement:** India suffers from a massive judicial backlog of **over 5 crore pending cases** (*National Judicial Data Grid*).
- **Potential of AI:** Artificial Intelligence offers assistive tools to enhance efficiency, speed, and administrative management.
- **Core Constraint:** However, the fundamental nature of justice demands that machines do not replace human judges!

*Introductory Principle:* AI cannot solve the core problem of adjudication. Adjudication must be conducted by humans because justice demands human thinking, empathy, and an ethical/moral compass. AI should play the role of an **assistive AI / judicial assistant**, improving efficiency without serving as the judge.

**Immediate Context / Trigger:**
1. **Supreme Court Draft Regulations:** Release of draft regulations for the use of AI in courts (2026).
2. **NCLT Fake Case Precedent:** Supreme Court raised alarm in a case before the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) where lawyers submitted fake, hallucinated legal precedents generated by ChatGPT LLMs. ChatGPT hallucinates fabricated judgments. If unverified, citing them in court damages judicial integrity. The Supreme Court observed that *"using AI-hallucinated material is like the release of toxic Methyl Isocyanate gas!"*

---

### Key Debates on AI in Judiciary

#### 1. Innovation vs. Judicial Independence
- **Need for Innovation:** Required to address massive delays and backlogs through automated **cause-list management**, case categorization, and improving the **Case Clearance Rate** (an Economic Survey metric tracking how fast old vs. new cases are disposed of).
- **Preserving Independence:** Judicial Independence is part of the **Basic Structure** of the Constitution. It must be protected to safeguard individual liberties and fundamental rights. Excessive reliance on unverified or hallucinating AI compromises judicial independence.

#### 2. Human Primacy vs. Algorithmic Justice
- **Problem with Algorithmic Justice:** AI algorithms look strictly for historical patterns and predictability. If historical data contains biases or discrimination, AI perpetuates them. 
- *Historical Analogy:* Similar to how the British labeled certain communities as "Criminal Tribes" based on historical bias, an AI fed with biased data might perform automated **risk scoring** and unfairly deny bail to underprivileged individuals while favoring wealthy litigants.
- **Solution:** Maintain **Human Primacy** in judicial decision-making with a **Human-in-the-Loop** model.

#### 3. Administrative AI vs. Adjudicative AI
- **Administrative / Clerical / Assistive AI:** Used for case management, transcription, translation, cause-list scheduling, and document processing. Highly desirable and necessary.
- **Adjudicative AI:** Using AI to determine guilt, pass judgments, or decide sentences. Highly problematic as it directly impacts fundamental rights.

#### 4. The Pyramid of AI Use in Judiciary

- **Green Level (Allowed):** Case management, cause-list automation, clerical tasks.
- **Amber Level (Cautious Use):** Legal research, transcription, translation. 
  - *Caution:* Language translation errors can be dangerous. The SC cited an instance where the legal phrase *"Leave granted"* was translated into Hindi as *"छुट्टी स्वीकार है"* (*"Holiday granted"*). In a UK case (*Noel v. UK*), the AI misread the surname "Noel" as "No", altering the outcome!
- **Red Level (Prohibited):** Automated adjudication and decision-making.

#### 5. Imported AI vs. Swadeshi Jurisprudence & Technological Sovereignty
- **The Dependency Risk:** Current frontier AI models are dominated by US tech giants. They train on Indian data, but control remains offshore. 
- **Technological Sovereignty:** India requires **Swadeshi AI models**, native datasets, and Swadeshi jurisprudence to ensure national data sovereignty in sensitive judicial matters.

---

### Essential Judicial Data Points (Must-Know for Mains)
1. **Pending Cases:** Over **5 crore pending cases** in India:
   - **Lower Judiciary:** ~4.3 to 4.4 crore cases (the biggest bottleneck).
   - **High Courts:** ~60 lakh cases.
   - **Supreme Court:** >80,000 cases.
2. **Judges-to-Population Ratio:** 
   - **India:** Currently **21 judges per million population** (Law Ministry data).
   - **Law Commission Recommendation (1987):** Recommended at least **50 judges per million population**.
   - **Global Benchmarks:** USA has **150 judges per million**; European countries average **220 judges per million**.

---

### Pros & Cons of AI in Judiciary

#### Pros (Assistive AI)
- **Administrative Efficiency:** Automated cause-list management, case categorization, tracking long-pending cases (5-10+ years).
- **Accessibility & Democratization:** Multilingual translation of judgments into regional languages, voice transcription, easy case status retrieval for citizens.
- **Advanced Legal Research:** Rapid summarization of massive case files, quick precedent retrieval (subject to human verification).

#### Cons & Major Issues (Adjudicative AI)
- **Judicial Hallucination:** Fabricated case laws (as in NCLT case). SC likened AI hallucinated data to a toxic gas leak.
- **Algorithmic Bias & Threat to Article 21:** Automated risk scoring targets vulnerable sections, violating Right to Life and Personal Liberty (*Article 21*).
- **Black Box AI:** Lack of transparency regarding how deep neural networks arrive at specific conclusions/predictions.
- **Accountability Vacuum:** When an AI error occurs, who is accountable? The developer, the government ministry, the lawyer, or the judge?
- **Constitutional Morality Violations:** Machines lack moral reasoning, empathy, and contextual understanding.

---

### General Implementation Challenges in India
1. **Digital Divide 1.0 vs. 2.0:**
   - **Digital Divide 1.0 (Access):** Physical availability of smartphones/internet — largely addressed in urban/semi-urban India.
   - **Digital Divide 2.0 (Literacy & Usage):** Inability to use tech safely, lack of cyber hygiene, vulnerability to cybercrimes (e.g., "Digital Arrests"), and unproductive exposure ("brain rot"). In courts, this creates structural barriers for digitally illiterate litigants.
2. **Data Protection:** Safeguarding sensitive personal case data under the **DPDP (Digital Personal Data Protection) Act**.
3. **Infrastructure Deficits:** Lack of Swadeshi datasets and localized AI models.

---

### Supreme Court Draft Regulations & Key Recommendations

#### Core Principles for Adoption:
1. **Human Primacy:** AI must function strictly in an assistive capacity; the human officer/judge retains ultimate decision-making power.
2. **Absolute Accountability:** Accountability rests entirely on the human judge/lawyer. One cannot claim *"ChatGPT made an error"* or blame algorithmic hallucination.
3. **Fairness & Non-Discrimination:** AI must not perpetuate historical biases or introduce new forms of discrimination.
4. **Transparency & Explainability:** Solves the Black Box AI problem; outputs must be comprehensible to judges, lawyers, and litigants.
5. **Inclusivity & Accessibility:** Usage must not widen the Digital Divide 2.0.

#### Prohibited Activities:
- Complete prohibition of **Algorithmic Adjudication / Decision-Making**.

#### Institutional Framework:
- **Apex Body:** A full-time regulator at the Supreme Court level to oversee AI integration.
- **Specialized Committees:** Judicial and Technical committees.
- **CORE AI:** Center of Research and Excellence on Artificial Intelligence.
- **Ethical Impact Assessment:** Mandatory evaluation of risks, vulnerabilities, and biases before piloting any AI tool in courts.
- **Human-in-the-Loop Model:** AI assists; humans supervise, verify, and decide.

---

### Landmark Quotes & Legal Precedents

#### Supreme Court Quotes:
- *"Relying on non-existent AI-generated judgments is not merely an error in decision-making, but amounts to judicial misconduct."* — **Supreme Court of India**
- *"Technology must augment, not replace, the human mind's discernment."* — **Supreme Court of India**
- *"The law, after all, is not a mere algorithm. It is the reflection of human conscience, shaped by empathy, moral reasoning, and an understanding of context that machines cannot replicate."* — **Supreme Court of India**
- *"Technology is only a tool; the heart of justice must remain human."* — **Supreme Court of India**

#### Landmark Case Laws & Scholars:
- ***Mata v. Avianca* (US District Court):** Landmark case where an attorney was heavily fined for submitting fake ChatGPT-generated case citations. Supreme Court of India reaffirmed a **Zero-Tolerance Policy** towards AI-generated fake citations.
- **Lee A. Bygrave / Scholars:** Advocate for **AI Literacy** and establishing the **Right of Litigants to Opt-Out** of AI-assisted court procedures.
- **Harvard Kennedy School Paper (*"AI, Judges, and Judgments"*):** Notes that *"public skepticism towards using AI in courtrooms is a healthy thing"* because skepticism enforces accountability.
- **China Model:** China experiments with AI-led decision-making strictly for **petty cases** (e.g., automated traffic fine disputes).

#### Existing Tech Initiatives in Indian Judiciary:
- **e-Courts Project:** Phase III ICT enablement across courts.
- **One Case One Data Platform:** Unified judicial data portal.
- **SUPACE (Supreme Court Portal for Assistance in Court's Efficiency):** AI workflow assistant for judges.
- **SuVas (Supreme Court Vidhik Anuvaad Software):** AI translation tool for translating judgments into regional languages.
- **SuSahayak:** AI-based conversational chatbot on the Supreme Court website.
- **Kerala High Court 24x7 On-Court:** Digital court for online processing of cheque bounce cases.

## 3. Public Distribution System (PDS) & Sarthak PDS

### Overview & Food Subsidy Context
**Instructor:** Public Distribution System (PDS) is directly in the UPSC Paper 3 syllabus. 

**Core Introduction:**
The Public Distribution System (PDS) is the backbone of India's food security, delivering subsidized foodgrains to **over 80 crore citizens**. However, the food subsidy budget burden is increasing every year:
- **Food Subsidy Estimate for FY27 (2026-27):** **₹2.28 lakh crore** (up from ₹2.03 lakh crore previously).
- **Sarthak PDS Context:** To address systemic leakages and rising subsidy costs, a technological overhaul through the **Sarthak PDS** umbrella scheme is being implemented to modernize the ecosystem.

---

### Definition of PDS
PDS is India's national food security initiative and a major social safety net managed jointly by the Center and States. It distributes essential food grains (rice, wheat) and non-food items (sugar, kerosene) to low-income populations at highly subsidized prices.

---

### Historical Evolution & Generations of Food Security

#### 1. Timeline of PDS Evolution
- **Pre-1960s (Urban Price Stabilization):** Meant primarily for urban consumers to stabilize food prices and combat food inflation during severe food scarcity (the "Ship-to-Mouth" era relying on US PL-480 imports).
- **1960s–1980s (Post-Green Revolution General Entitlement):** Expanded into a universal general entitlement scheme providing subsidized foodgrains to all citizens.
- **1990s (RPDS & TPDS):**
  - *Revamped PDS (RPDS - 1992):* Focused on remote, hilly, and drought-prone areas.
  - *Targeted PDS (TPDS - 1997):* Shifted from universal entitlement to targeted delivery for Below Poverty Line (BPL) families.
- **2000 (Antyodaya Anna Yojana - AAY):** Launched to target the "poorest of the poor" with highly subsidized foodgrains (₹2/kg wheat, ₹3/kg rice).
- **2013 (National Food Security Act - NFSA):** Converted welfare assistance into a **statutory legal right** covering **67% of the population** (75% rural and 50% urban).
- **Post-2020 (PMGKAY & Modernization):** Launched Pradhan Mantri Garib Kalyan Anna Yojana during Covid-19 and shifted focus towards total digitization, automation, and supply chain tracking.

#### 2. Three Generations of Food Security
```
 Generation 1: AVAILABILITY   ---> Green Revolution (Focus on aggregate food production)
 Generation 2: AFFORDABILITY  ---> TPDS, NFSA (Focus on access, pricing & legal entitlement)
 Generation 3: SUSTAINABILITY ---> Sarthak PDS, Tech Modernization, Climate Smart Ag (Focus on efficiency, zero leakage)
```
- **1st Generation (Availability):** Addressed absolute food deficit through the Green Revolution.
- **2nd Generation (Affordability & Accessibility):** Addressed access through TPDS, NFSA, and price subsidies.
- **3rd Generation (Sustainability & Modernization):** Focuses on eliminating leakages, smart logistics, climate-resilient agriculture, and technological intelligence per acre.

---

### Institutional Framework: Center vs. State Roles

```
  CENTER (FCI)                        STATE MACHINERY                    BENEFICIARY
 [Procurement &]  ---> Intra-State ---> [District -> Block ->] ---> [Fair Price Shop] ---> Citizen
 [ Bulk Storage]       Logistics        [ Local Warehouses  ]       [  (ePOS & Aadhaar) ]
```

#### Center (Food Corporation of India - FCI):
- Bulk procurement of foodgrains at Minimum Support Price (MSP).
- Inter-state transportation and bulk storage in FCI godowns.
- *Structural Deficit:* FCI relies heavily on **Centralized Procurement**, requiring massive storage infrastructure. Due to storage deficits, FCI uses **CAP (Cover and Plinth)** storage — stacking grain sacks on open outdoor platforms covered only by tarpaulin sheets. This results in heavy losses from rain and pests, which officials routinely blame on "rats" to cover up administrative theft!

#### State Governments:
- Beneficiary identification and issuance of Ration Cards.
- Intra-state transportation (from state godowns -> district -> block -> Fair Price Shop).
- Licensing and oversight of **Fair Price Shops (FPS)**.
- Ensuring viable dealer commissions (dealer margins) to make FPS operations sustainable.

---

### The Sarthak PDS Scheme
**Full Form:** *Scheme for Assistance in Ration Transport and Handling Income with Automation in PDS*.

- **Umbrella Integration:** Clubs intra-state transport financial assistance and **Smart PDS** (digitization) into a single unified framework.
- **Financial Outlay:** **₹25,530 crore** allocated for implementation.
- **Key Objectives:**
  1. Financial support to states for intra-state foodgrain logistics.
  2. Ensuring timely FPS dealer margins to prevent dealer-level diversion.
  3. End-to-end supply chain tracking from FCI godowns to FPS counters.
  4. Complete beneficiary digitization and grievance redressal mechanism.

---

### Essential PDS Data Points (Must-Know for Mains)
1. **Financial Outlay:** ₹25,530 crore for Sarthak PDS.
2. **Target Demography:** Over **80 crore citizens** covered under NFSA/PMGKAY.
3. **FCI Excess Buffer Stocks:** As of January 2026, FCI held **309 lakh tonnes of rice** — **9 times higher than required buffer norms!**
4. **ICRIER Leakage Study:** An ICRIER (*Indian Council for Research on International Economic Relations*) study revealed that **28% of grains** distributed through PDS fail to reach intended beneficiaries due to leakages and diversion.
5. **Digitization Progress (Economic Survey):**
   - **99.8% of Ration Cards** are Aadhaar-seeded.
   - **99.6% of Fair Price Shops** are equipped with **ePOS (Electronic Point of Sale)** devices.

### Expert Views, Committees & Case Studies

#### 1. Shanta Kumar Committee (2014) Recommendations:
- Unbundle FCI operations into procurement, storage, and distribution.
- Outsource stocking and warehousing to private/CWC/SWC entities.
- Introduce Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT) in major cities and grain-surplus states.
- Reduce NFSA coverage from 67% to 40% to streamline subsidy burden.

#### 2. Prof. P. Balakrishnan View:
- PDS serves as an **equalizing consumption platform** across diverse Indian states.
- Functions as a vital **crisis shock absorber** during external economic shocks (e.g., West Asia conflict, Russia-Ukraine war, Covid-19 supply disruptions).

---

### Core Challenges in PDS
1. **Capacity Deficits:** Lack of administrative, technical, and logistical capacity at state and block levels.
2. **Inclusion & Exclusion Errors:** Eligible poor/migrants excluded while non-needy households hold cards.
3. **Storage Deficits & CAP Losses:** Open-air storage deterioration and physical theft.
4. **Dealer Margin Viability:** Low margins lead shop owners to divert grain into the open market.
